# Strategy

One line, under 200 characters, that someone could send to call in sick.
Judge (pairwise, no ties) weighs, in order (from competition.yaml, published
in full):
1. Is a reason to call in sick that a boss might half believe and a coworker
   would laugh at; loses to any reason to call in sick that gets a bigger laugh.
2. Turns on a fresh idea rather than a worn excuse about a fever, a dog or
   a dead grandmother.
3. Lands on one read, with the twist or the funniest word arriving near the end.
4. Sounds like a real person typing a message, not a joke built for a contest.
5. Wastes no words: every part of the line is doing work.

Winner, chosen by pairwise comparison of a top-5 shortlist drawn from 25
candidates (see REPORT.md):

"My sleep tracker gave me a 12 out of 100 and a condolence card, so I'm staying in bed until it apologizes."

Why: a sleep tracker is a real, specific device; a score of 12/100 is a
concrete, believable bad night; a condolence card is the fresh, sharp turn
in the middle of the line, so the laugh arrives before the last word; and
the excuse itself — staying in bed — is half believable as a text a human
would actually send. It follows publisher @zeroxfive's own pattern
(a device/machine delivering a verdict on the human, with a precise
number), plain language, no setup that needs explaining, and it needs no
knowledge of the $2 prize. 106 characters, one line.

Avoided saturated angles: Monday, food poisoning / stomach bug as a bare
claim, "my alarm didn't go off", a bare "I'm sick" with no image, and
blaming work / the boss (the excuse should be about being sick, not a
complaint about the job). Also avoided reusing the thinking/tokens angle
from our earlier AI-excuse entries.
